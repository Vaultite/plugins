Tab rail puts your open tabs down the right edge of the window, as a browser's vertical tabs do, and gives the page everything else.

## Where things are
- **Tabs** are in the right sidebar, top to bottom; **your favourites** are the tiles above them (right-click a tab, Pin, to add its page). A tab you haven't looked at for 12 hours is archived, so the list stays short: find it again in ⌘T.
- **No headings**: the panels have only a little room between them. Point at that room, or hold ⌥, and its name and buttons show; right-click a panel for its menu (fold it, give it its heading back, move it).
- **Search and your files** are in the left sidebar. Hide it (⌘\ or the button at its top) and the page takes its place; it slides back in while the pointer is at the window's left edge.
- **⌘T** opens a bar: an address, words to search the web, or the name of a note, a tab or a bookmark.
- **Glance**: ⌥-click a link to look at it over the page, then open it or close it and carry on.

## Making it yours
Each part is a setting: Quiet panel headings and the sidebars' other switches in Settings, Sidebars; how long tabs wait before they're archived in Tabs' settings; the tab bar and the colours in Appearance. Your old layout is a click away in Settings, Appearance (or Restore previous layout in the command palette), and this guide is yours to edit or delete.
